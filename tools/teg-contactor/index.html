Why some inputs are typed
Sliders carry the operating case. Typed fields are the ones you move less: the spec is a contract value, and lean purity and circulation move diameter less than 4% across their ranges. Out-of-range entries are refused, not clamped. Gas composition is fixed (illustrative, SG 0.633) until it's set.
Internals
Method
MOC methodology, segmented by component (Step 1a): the shell is carbon steel with a corrosion allowance; the packing is stainless, because thin packing sheet can't carry a corrosion allowance (Step 6) and CO₂ in the gas (composition still open) is the governing unknown (Step 2). See the MOC panel. For random packing the material sets the minimum wetting rate (Liquid Wetting Rates: Table 9-25 when the MOC is listed).
Foaming system factor (flood derating) · packings
Method
Perry's 9th Table 14-9 lists glycol contactors as heavy-foaming: 0.73 (ref 1), 0.65 (refs 3, 4), 0.50 (ref 2: "0.65 for glycol contactors in synthesis gas service, 0.5 for others"). Natural gas is "others", so packings default to 0.50. Bubble-cap trays default to 0.73: with it the AIChE tray efficiency lands at about GPSA's 25–30% field values, where 0.50 over-predicts it (about 38% at the base case). Each family keeps its own factor; this control edits the one shown. Fair's tray flood correlation is valid only for "low or nonfoaming" systems without it, and Perry's applies the same derating to packings. The overview reports diameter across all three values.
Structured packing
Method
Every Perry's 9th Table 14-14 packing with a packing factor, sized at this case (Kister–Gill capacity, Eq 14-160 HETP) and ranked by favorability. No source gives a structured size-selection procedure; Ludwig p.342: "several competitive designs are often helpful for a final design selection", which is what the ranking does. Perry's p.14-52: finer corrugations are more efficient but have lower capacity and more plugging and fouling sensitivity. ★ = field-tested in TEG service (ARCO, OGJ 1991).

Method
AIChE Bubble Tray Design Manual (Perry's 9th Eqs 13-72 to 13-78) → point efficiency → Lewis case 1 EMV (Eq 14-137) → EOC (Eq 14-135). O'Connell (Fig 14-9) is off chart for TEG and kept for reference. Perry's warns that fundamental methods tend to over-predict.

Liquid distributor
Method
Liquid Distributor Type Selection (Ludwig Table 9-18, Bonilla 1993). Auto rule: drip-point types only, because in TEG service a high-efficiency drip-point distributor was "far superior to a notched trough or spray nozzle distributor" (Ludwig Vol 2 p.342, Dean et al.; tested on structured packing). Then, proposed: rated good or better "at low liquid rates", solids handling at or above what the fouling tier needs, best uniformity, lowest cost.
Distribution quality
Method
Perry's 9th p.14-67 (Moore & Rukovena): quality rating 10–70% for standard commercial distributors, 75–90% intermediate, over 90% high-performance. Seider's distributor cost is for high-performance units, so that is the default.

Random packing model
Method
Every Perry's 8th Table 14-13 packing with a packing factor, ranked by favorability at this case. Hydraulics: the deck's Leva route where Ludwig Table 9-33A has constants, otherwise the Kister–Gill/Strigle GPDC (Perry's Fig 14-55, proposed deck step). Flood prediction
Method
